## The opportunity
An overseas business could explore a small-group cultural food experience with an existing local provider. Tsuzuki Shoten is an actual example: the prefectural tourism website describes grinding buckwheat, making noodles and eating them at its Higashi-Iya premises. This is evidence of an existing experience, not proof of availability for partnership or unmet demand. [Official listing](https://www.awanavi.jp/archives/spot/1990)

## Proposed plan
Six guests, at an existing provider's premises, with the local provider proposed to run the workshop. No arranged transport or accommodation. This is a fictional proposal; the provider has not agreed. Seller, payment and contractual roles remain open. Costs are outside scope.

## Consultation brief
“We are exploring a six-person soba workshop at an existing local provider's venue. No transport or accommodation is arranged. Seller and payment roles are not yet agreed. Please confirm whether the proposed cooking, eating and any take-home food are covered by the venue's arrangements, what further checks are needed, and who should handle them.”

## Next actions
1. Confirm provider interest and commercial venue terms.
2. Record who sells, receives payment, teaches and handles food.
3. Use that outline for the health and travel-business enquiries.
4. Revisit the checks if transport, lodging, venue or food handling changes.

The sample is complete as a consultation-preparation product. It is not a permit, a partner agreement or a full legal review. No party has been contacted. A new venue or construction would require a separate scope.
